This entry sets out to give skoro the uniform distribution on its compact types, the rotations and
the spheres among them, whose reference measure is finite and so normalizes to a law; and to say
what a normal with a broad scale is on a curved type. [The normal](../normal/) recorded that, under
the Cayley chart of the rotations or the stereographic chart of the sphere, its mean is a mode only
while the isotropic scale is below one in the chart's coordinates, and that a broader one piles its
mass near the half-turns or the antipode, where the chart compresses most. A normal made broad to
say that little is known of a rotation or a direction therefore says something else, and the law
that does say it is the uniform one. The entry revises the normal's story on that point.
